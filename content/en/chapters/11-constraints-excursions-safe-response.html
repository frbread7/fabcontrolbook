Control Constraints, Excursions, and Safe Response

Learning objectives

Separate statistical signals from specification violations and equipment or personnel hazards. Define constraints before selecting an automatic or manual response. Build an excursion workflow that preserves evidence, contains risk, and avoids converting an uncertain alarm into an unjustified recipe change.

“Excursion” is an operational label, not a statistical diagnosis. It may describe a control-chart signal, a sensor limit, a product measurement outside a specification, or a safety condition; each demands a different authority and response. A common case record should identify what crossed which limit, when, the affected population, data quality, and the person or function empowered to act.

Constraint layers and response hierarchy

Keep at least three boundaries distinct. A control limit is estimated from a process baseline and indicates unusual behavior under the chart’s assumptions. A product or process specification expresses an acceptance requirement. An equipment operating or safety limit protects a tool, process, or person. An alarm threshold may be an earlier decision point selected to allow investigation or intervention. These values can differ and must have documented owners, units, applicability, and version. A control chart can signal while all product values remain in specification; an individual product can fail specification without a prior chart signal.

For a bounded manipulated variable, one simple constraint is umin ≤ uk ≤ umax, possibly with a move bound |uk−uk−1| ≤ Δumax. Those are mathematical constraints, not proof that the whole operating envelope is safe. Multivariable constraints can conflict, model uncertainty can make predicted feasibility optimistic, and hard safety interlocks must remain independent of a performance optimizer. When an optimizer has no feasible move, the correct state is an explicit infeasibility/hold response, not silent clipping followed by a claim that control succeeded.

A practical response hierarchy is: validate the signal and context; notify the responsible operator or engineer; establish the affected lot, wafer, chamber, and time window; apply pre-authorized containment or hold rules; investigate competing causes; disposition material through the defined quality authority; and release only after stated evidence is met. Avoid irreversible changes while the measurement chain, recipe revision, or tool state is uncertain. Preserve original readings and corrected readings separately, including the reason, author, and timestamp of any correction.

Safety-related functions require a hazard analysis and lifecycle governed by the responsible organization’s applicable requirements. A process monitor, SPC chart, or advisory APC controller is not automatically a safety instrumented function. Do not credit it as a protective layer without the required independence, reliability evidence, verification, and authority. For high-consequence events, follow site emergency procedures and qualified safety personnel rather than an educational chart interpretation.

Worked example: signal without a specification failure

A synthetic thickness monitor has a baseline mean of 50.0 nm and estimated standard deviation 0.20 nm. A three-sigma Shewhart upper control limit is 50.6 nm when the individual-measurement assumptions and stable baseline are acceptable. The product specification is independently set at 49.0–51.0 nm. A result of 50.7 nm triggers the chart but remains within specification. This is evidence of unusual process behavior, not by itself proof of an out-of-spec wafer or a cause.

First check unit, calibration, sample identity, tool/chamber, and whether the baseline spans comparable states. Then determine the potentially affected run interval and use the approved hold/notification procedure. Do not move a recipe merely to pull the next point under the control limit: that can hide the signal, create a second shift, or cross an equipment constraint. If a product measurement later exceeds 51.0 nm, quality disposition follows the applicable specification and material-control system, even if the chart had not yet signaled. The values are invented solely to illustrate distinct decisions.

Limits of automated response

Thresholds can be wrong because of stale baselines, autocorrelation, product mix, sensor drift, or multiple testing. A “safe” numerical action cannot be inferred from a chart alone. Define authority boundaries, manual fallback, maximum data age, alarm escalation, and recovery criteria. Never bypass equipment interlocks or use this chapter as a process-specific containment instruction. Safety, quality, and performance actions require qualified local procedures and responsible approval.
