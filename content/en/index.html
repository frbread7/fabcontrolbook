What this book teaches

A process-control system is more than a chart or threshold. It is a chain from a physical process and sensor, through data identity and statistical reasoning, to an alarm and a safe, reviewed response. This text treats that chain as an engineering design problem.

Four learning blocks

01–03

Architecture and evidence

Context, data lineage, measurement, sampling, and uncertainty.

04–08

Statistical and equipment monitoring

Chart design, detection, trace features, multivariate monitoring, and tool matching.

09–12

Feedback and response

Run-to-run control, delays, constraints, alarms, and disposition.

13–14

Integrated practice

Synthetic excursion investigations, verification, and governance.

Related My Library books

Use WATBook for parametric measurement and measurement-to-decision fundamentals. Use ProcessIntegrationBook for cross-module integration. This book assumes those foundations where helpful and concentrates on operational monitoring and control loops.

Model and data boundary

Every dataset in the labs and case studies is synthetic and identified as such. The models are illustrative and receive deterministic numerical checks; they are not calibrated to a fab, validated on production data, or approved for process-control use.

How to use this text

Follow the chapters in order for a complete course, or enter through the topic you need. Read the model assumptions before interpreting an alarm or controller result. The interactive activities are optional complements; they do not replace engineering review, local change control, or equipment safety procedures.
